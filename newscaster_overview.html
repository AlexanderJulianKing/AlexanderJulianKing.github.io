Newscaster

How an automated daily news show is researched, fact-checked, and published every morning

I built Newscaster to research, write, fact-check, and publish a daily news episode with no one at the keyboard. I run each lead story through a research loop I designed, with an adversary model that looks for counter-evidence, and every script is checked against its sources before it is voiced. What past episodes reported is carried forward by an embedding-based memory. More than 525 episodes have been published to YouTube.

(1)

The pipeline

i

Six stages, every morning, with no one at the keyboard.

  1. Gather

    Read about 150 headlines, pick 2 stories to go deep on and 5 to cover briefly, and research them.

  2. Write scripts

    The two deep dives are written as conversations between an anchor and a reporter, and the other five as a short roundup.

  3. Fact-check

    Check every quote and claim against the sources, and fix what is wrong before anything is voiced.

  4. Audio

    A different voice for each speaker, plus theme music.

  5. Video

    The audio over the cover image.

  6. Upload

    Published to YouTube.

This workflow runs for each of the two deep-dive picks, and the results are then saved and indexed.

Workflow 3 · answers only from verified sources

Grounded search

“Grounded” means tied to real fetched sources, never the model’s own memory. The first pass and the research agent’s source-checking tool (the “source hunter”) both use this workflow. Its answers come only from verified excerpts, and when there are none, it reports no evidence instead of guessing.

Each page is checked in code for the right date, people, and topic. For news, the pages are ranked against the checklist instead of rejected by it, because a strict checklist was discarding real coverage of breaking stories. If no page passes on the first run, the whole flow runs again with the model reasoning harder. Every run is logged, and the saved excerpts are what the fact-finder later checks against.

Workflow 4 · the dig-deeper loop

Research agent

The agent starts from the story’s research so far: the article summaries plus the grounded search’s first findings. The orchestrator (Opus) then keeps deciding whether to dig further or stop, calling one of two tools each round, until a second model (GPT-6 Sol), acting as an adversary that looks for holes in the findings, signs off. It runs up to 8 rounds and stops early after 2 rounds that turn up nothing new.

One tool is the source hunter (workflow 3), which answers only from verified sources; the other runs a fresh web search and pulls in another relevant article. The adversary checks once. If it finds a gap, the agent digs one more round; otherwise the agent hands its findings back to Gather.

Workflow 5 · what past episodes already covered

Embedding-based RAG memory

This retrieval system keeps a record of what past episodes covered. Each note is turned into a vector, a list of numbers that represents its meaning, and stored. A new story’s question is turned into a vector too, and the most similar past notes are returned.

The memory is there for continuity: building on a running story, not repeating what was already covered, and finding fresh angles. Notes are tagged by storyline, and if nothing matches, the story starts fresh. In a leakage-free chronological replay, the right prior coverage ranked first in 12 of 12 recurring-story tests, and 93% of the top six results were relevant.

Workflow 6 · the last check before voicing

The fact-finder

This is the last fact-check before the episode is voiced and published. Every script is checked against the source text the pipeline actually fetched (the saved excerpts), not against the LLM summaries, which would carry an invented claim straight through. There are three passes, each aimed at a different way fabrication gets in: a quote check (are quoted words verbatim?), a faithfulness check (does every claim match its source?), and a stable-fact check (are names and titles right?). It runs on every script, the overview and the segments, just before text-to-speech.

Pass 2 runs on GPT-6 Luna at medium reasoning (GLM 5.2 until September 2026) against only that segment’s sources. The tight context is what lets it catch a subtle contradiction that a long, noisy corpus would bury. In the June 23, 2026 episode, it flagged the script’s “a crushing defeat in the Makerfield by-election” when the sources say Andy Burnham won it with 55%. Only the source-grounded flags, from passes 1 and 2, go to the editor (workflow 7). The stable-fact pass stays advisory, because its web check is unreliable on facts newer than the model’s training; in the same episode it insisted the Fed chair was still Powell and dismissed the 2026 dates as “simulated.” Its flags are logged but never auto-edited, and no flag ever blocks the run.

Workflow 7 · fix the confirmed errors before voicing

The fact-finder editor

The editor runs only on scripts with a source-grounded flag (quote or faithfulness). Opus proposes verbatim find-and-replace fixes for the confirmed factual errors, a GPT-6 Sol adversary signs off on each one, and plain code applies a fix only where the exact phrase occurs once. It runs up to 3 rounds.

The reference is that segment’s fetched excerpts. The adversary rejects anything uncertain or unsupported, so only checkable, contradicted facts are changed; ambiguous spans and everything else stay as logged flags.

A story through the machine

iii

This follows one real story from the June 23, 2026 episode: the U.K. leadership shake-up. The source hunter got it right, then the cheaper rewrite model inverted the outcome, calling Andy Burnham’s by-election landslide a “crushing defeat.” The faithfulness pass caught the flip, and the editor restored the win before voicing.

The models named here are the ones that ran that day (Gemma, GLM 5.2, and GPT-5.5); GPT-6 Luna and GPT-6 Sol do those jobs now.

Episode stages 4–6 · audio → video → upload

Anchor: …After devastating losses in May and rival Andy Burnham’s crushing victory in the Makerfield by-election on June nineteenth, Starmer decided it was time to step down…

The corrected read is voiced (an anchor and two reporters), turned into video, and uploaded to YouTube as a public episode, alongside the day’s deep dives (“Why a judge quashed DOJ subpoenas targeting Minnesota leaders,” “Why the Supreme Court reinstated Pedro Hernandez’s convictions”).

Each grounded fix is recorded in the edit log with the source behind it. The Warsh flag stays logged but untouched, because the stable-fact pass is advisory.

Newscaster is deployed and runs daily on a Raspberry Pi, a credit-card-sized computer; each run starts at 4 AM. Labels, models, and sources reflect the codebase as of September 23, 2026, except the research agent’s round limit, which was raised to 8 on September 25.
