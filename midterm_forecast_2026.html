How the forecast works

Our forecast combines public polls, past results, facts about each race such as incumbency, and published expert ratings, then simulates the election many thousands of times. At its core is a Bayesian model: it starts from what past elections suggest and updates that with every poll.

Our model

  • Every poll is its own reading of the race’s true margin: what a poll with no error would find that day. Each pollster’s lean is measured from its polls this year. Party or campaign polls and registered-voter polls are adjusted by amounts measured in past elections.
  • Each race starts from the state's or district's partisan lean and incumbency, moved by the national environment, then follows its polls.
  • Polling errors are simulated at three levels: one shared nationwide, one shared within each state, and one for each race, with room for large misses.
  • Prediction-market prices are never an input.

Expert ratings

  • House: each rated seat takes its odds from its Cook, Sabato and Inside Elections ratings, the approach that tested best on past elections. Our model decides how the seats move together. A cap limits how far the ratings can pull the overall House result away from our model.
  • Senate: our model alone, with no expert ratings.
  • Governors: our model alone, except in races with two polls or fewer, where the forecast moves halfway toward the expert ratings.

Data

  • Polls, each counted only from the time it became public: Wikipedia poll tables as they stood on each date, plus a daily feed of poll aggregators’ listings.
  • National generic ballot (polls asking which party people will vote for in their House race): RealClearPolling, as saved by the Internet Archive.
  • Presidential approval: Gallup for 1948–2024. For 2026, polls listed by RealClearPolling (as saved by the Internet Archive), adjusted to Gallup’s scale.
  • Expert ratings, from Wikipedia as it stood on each date.
  • Tested by replaying the 2002–2024 elections, each with only the information available at the time.
