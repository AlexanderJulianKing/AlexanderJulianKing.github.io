Why these three scenarios?

The 5.5-day gap establishes that the cadence changed; it does not reveal whether the slower rate now holds or keeps slowing. The default puts 80% on continued slowing, 15% on holding, and 5% on renewed acceleration. That is an explicit prior stance, not an objective probability learned from four gaps.

This mix implies a 23% chance the next gap is shorter than 5.5 days.

Target
The timing model predicts the next public 11M announcement, measured from the 10M post. The separate reset estimate asks whether Tibo resets limits when that post appears.
Why three scenarios
The 9M→10M interval took 5.5 days, so the average signup rate in that window was about a fifth of the early-July rate. The data show that change but cannot tell whether the slower pace now reverses, holds, or keeps lengthening.
Why those defaults
The first three million-user gaps were about a day each; the latest took 5.5 days. The controls start at 5% for a return to the early pace, 15% for the latest pace holding, and 80% for another slowdown. That reflects the view that another growth burst would probably need a new launch. These are adjustable assumptions, not probabilities proven by four gaps.
Why 1.8, 5.5, and 9.4 days
1.8 days is a conservative partial return toward the roughly one-day early-July cadence, rather than assuming the initial burst resumes in full. 5.5 days repeats the latest observed 9M→10M gap. 9.4 days continues the multiplicative lengthening of the observed gaps, but is slightly less extreme than the raw trend extrapolation of roughly 9.7 days.
Survival update
When no public post has appeared, dates that have already passed are removed automatically and the remaining scenario weights are recalculated.
No-post outcome
A default 5% structural chance represents no milestone post within 60 days. It gains conditional weight during prolonged silence and keeps the chart from promising an announcement with certainty.
Why a lognormal wait
The wait cannot be negative, and proportional misses are more plausible than equal hour-by-hour errors: taking twice as long is comparable to taking half as long. A lognormal distribution captures that while leaving room for an unusually delayed post.
Reset estimate
Four of five milestone posts triggered immediate global resets. To avoid treating five observations as certainty, the calculation begins by giving every reset rate from 0% to 100% equal plausibility—called a Beta(1,1), or uniform Beta, prior. After the four observed resets, the estimated chance next time is 71%, but remains weakly known, with a 36–96% interval. The weekly-renewal planner multiplies the chance of an 11M post before your renewal by 71%, assuming reset policy is independent of timing.
